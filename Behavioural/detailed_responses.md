## WATOnomous

### Tell me about a time you took ownership of a robotics problem that was not well defined.

At WATOnomous, we had a robotic arm in simulation, but no practical way to control it for manipulation tasks. The problem was not well defined because there was no established pipeline, just the goal of making the arm respond to natural operator motion. I took ownership of that by doing the research myself, reading papers, and figuring out how to use MediaPipe hand tracking to generate ROS commands through rosbridge into the simulator. The first-principles challenge was to build a simple, reliable path from human motion to robot motion instead of overcomplicating the interface. I got that pipeline working, which let us mimic human arm motion in sim and use it for manipulation tasks. What I took from that experience is that when a problem is ambiguous, I try to define the missing system boundaries and build the first workable version quickly.

### Tell me about a time your simulation work did not transfer cleanly to the real robot. What did you do?

One clear example was a robot controller that worked well in simulation but behaved poorly on the real system. In sim, the robot was responsive and smooth, but once we ran it on real motors, the motion became jerky and unstable because the physical system had delays and dynamics the simulator was not capturing well. I approached it from first principles and focused on the actual failure mode, which was overly aggressive acceleration causing wheel drift and spin-out. I added damping during faster acceleration to smooth the command profile and make the behavior more realistic for hardware. That improved control stability and made the system transfer much better from sim to real. It reinforced for me that simulation is useful, but hardware exposes dynamics you cannot ignore.

### Describe a situation where you had to debug a robotics issue under time pressure.

At WATOnomous, a teammate got sick right before a deadline, so I had to take over part of their work on an IK controller in Isaac Sim. I realized solving for the full Jacobian and full pose was adding complexity without helping the immediate goal, so I switched to a more reliable offline MuJoCo-based solver and simplified it to position-only IK. That change gave us near-perfect convergence in time for the deadline. The main lesson was that under pressure, the best move is often to simplify the problem to the smallest thing that must work correctly.
